# Tent Platform — Bill of Materials (₱10,000 DIY build)

**Build:** 3.0 × 3.0 m elevated tent deck, 8 ft (2.44 m) to deck top, on 4 coco-lumber posts inset 500 mm from each edge (2,000 × 2,000 mm grid). Ridge beam across the centre for an inverted-V tarp. No railing. Open silong underneath.
**Location:** Batangas City, Philippines. **Labour:** ₱0 — DIY, ~6–8 weekends.
**Prices:** gathered 16 Sep 2026, indicative ranges — verify at your lumberyard.

> **Why coco lumber and not 4" PVC + concrete?** Four 3 m lengths of 4" PVC plus 10 bags of cement, 8 lengths of rebar, aggregates and tie wire is **₱8,800–14,000 for the four posts alone** — the whole budget, with nothing left for a 9 m² deck. A 4 × 4 coco post carries ~5 tonnes once knee-braced and each post only sees ~550 kg. You lose longevity (10–15 yr vs 30+), not safety. The concrete columns are **Phase 4**.

**If you come in over ₱10,000**, trim in this order: ① decking to 1 × 6 with wider gaps (−₱400) · ② joists to 450 mm centres (−₱330) · ③ deck to 2.7 × 2.7 m (−₱1,100).

### ⭐ Alternative: build 2.4 × 2.4 m instead — **₱8,030**

Same design, smaller deck, still fits a 2-person tent with a walk margin. Leaves **₱1,970** — enough for Phase 3, so you get **the platform *and* the tarp inside the same ₱10,000**. Take this if you want rain cover from day one.

## Shopping notes

- Lumber, cement, aggregates, rebar, bolts → **local lumberyard + hardware, in person.** Coco lumber grading varies hugely between yards; the four posts and two beams must be picked by hand.
- Worth buying on Shopee: [wood preservative](https://da.gd/ouTJOU) · [trapal](https://da.gd/TvfwY) · [nylon rope](https://da.gd/tn58xq) · [turnbuckles](https://da.gd/qhdGi) · [eye bolts](https://da.gd/6lV9I)
